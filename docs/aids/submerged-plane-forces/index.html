Forces on submerged plane surfaces

Centre of pressure

The force does not act at the centroid.

Pressure grows with depth, so the lower half of a plate is pushed harder than the upper half. The resultant therefore acts below the centroid — at the centre of pressure. How far below is the whole question.

k² is the only thing about the shape that the answer depends on. Two plates of equal area and different k² give the same force in different places.

What k² actually is

k² = I/A is the mean square distance of the area from the centroid: average every strip's distance squared, weighted by how much area sits there. It is a variance. So the offset k²/yC* reads as variance of depth over mean depth, and both limits follow without algebra. Spread the area further from its own centre and the offset grows; push the plate deeper and the mean swamps the variance, so CP collapses onto C.
