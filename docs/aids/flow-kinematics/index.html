The field

Every point in the region has a velocity vector and a pressure. The assignment of those values to points is the field. No particle is being followed on this panel.

At the origin both velocity components vanish, so V = 0. This is the stagnation point. It is also the point of highest pressure.

Constants. V0 = 1 m/s, ℓ = 1 m, so k = V0/ℓ = 1 s−1. Air at ρ = 1.2 kg/m3. Pressure from p = p0 − ½ρk2r2, exact for this field.

This flow is steady, so ∂V/∂t = 0 at every point. Particles still accelerate.

The amber term is zero at every point and every instant in this flow. The particle still accelerates. It slows as it approaches the stagnation region, turns, and speeds up again on the way out. All of that comes from the teal term.

Steady means that nothing changes at a fixed point. It does not mean that nothing accelerates. The material derivative has two terms for this reason.

For this field the convective term equals k2 times the offset from the stagnation point, so it points directly away from it at every location.

Let the oncoming wind veer up and down, so the stagnation point slides along the face of the sign: vy = k(y − ys), ys = A sin ωt. The sign does not move. The temporal term is now non-zero.

With the amplitude above zero the stagnation point moves up and down the face, so the streamline pattern is no longer fixed in space. The amber arrow points purely along y, because only vy carries the time dependence. ax remains purely convective at every amplitude.

Steady flow. A particle released at the source follows the streamline, and every later particle follows the same one, so the three curves coincide. This identity holds only in steady flow.

Unsteady flow. The streamline is defined at one instant, the pathline records the history of one particle, and the streakline is the current position of all particles released from the source. The three are different curves.

The timeline is a row of particles tagged at one instant, dashed at the tagged positions and solid at the current positions. It rotates toward the face of the sign, stretches, and remains straight. This velocity field is linear in x and y, so it stretches and rotates material lines without bending them. That is linear strain, with no shear and no rotation.

A fixed sensor and a tagged particle in the same flow, both recording speed. The difference between the two records is the convective term.

At zero gust amplitude the probe trace is flat, because nothing changes at a fixed point in a steady flow. The particle trace falls and then rises, because the particle is carried into slow fluid near the stagnation point and out into fast fluid again.

A neutrally buoyant weather balloon corresponds to the amber trace: it moves with the air and records what a fluid element experiences. A mast-mounted anemometer corresponds to the teal trace.

Both records are correct. They measure different quantities, related by D/Dt = ∂/∂t + V·∇.
