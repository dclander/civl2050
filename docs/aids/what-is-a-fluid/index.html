What makes a fluid

Step 1 — a solid under shear

A solid reaches a static equilibrium under shear.

Bond a block of steel between two plates and drag the top plate sideways. The block distorts through an angle and then holds there, at a static equilibrium. The angle is set by the stress and not by elapsed time.

Step 2 — a fluid under shear

A fluid reaches no equilibrium under shear.

Replace the steel with water and drag the same way. There is no angle at which it settles. The distortion grows for as long as the stress acts, so time enters the problem, and the measurable quantity is the rate of strain rather than the strain.

Step 4 — the definition that falls out

A fluid cannot resist a shear stress at rest.

A fluid is not defined by being wet, or by taking the shape of its container. Those are consequences. The definition is a statement about the response to shear.

SOLID

τ = Gγ
  • Stress sets the strain.
  • A static equilibrium exists: it distorts, then stops.
  • Time is absent from the law.
  • Release it and it returns.

FLUID

τ = μ dγ/dt
  • Stress sets the strain rate.
  • No equilibrium exists: it never stops.
  • Time appears in the law.
  • Release it and it stays where it was left.

Because no angle balances the stress, the fluid keeps deforming for as long as the stress is there. That continuous deformation is flow. Continuous deformation under shear is what defines a fluid.

So which phases are fluids?

Apply the test: can it hold a shear stress without moving?

SOLID Yes: not a fluid.
LIQUID No — a fluid.
GAS No — a fluid.

Then what separates liquid from gas?

Cohesion between molecules. This is a separate question, answered after both have been classified as fluids.

  • Liquid: strong cohesion; keeps its volume, takes the container’s shape, forms a free surface under gravity.
  • Gas: weak cohesion; fills the whole container, no free surface.

Numbers used: structural steel G = 79.3 GPa, shear yield τy ≈ 0.577 σy ≈ 144 MPa for A36; water μ = 1.0 × 10⁻³ Pa·s at 20 °C. The plate demonstration uses a 1 mm gap at 1 m/s, giving dγ/dt = V/h = 1000 s⁻¹.
